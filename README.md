# The 3.5% — National Renewal Fund

**A grassroots campaign for a permanent UK National Renewal Fund.**

The National Renewal Fund (NRF) is a proposal to turn a finite period of contribution from extreme private wealth and the UK's largest corporate economic footprints into productive national assets.

The first investment programme would focus on energy generation, storage and supporting infrastructure. Returns from those assets would be retained and reinvested so that the Fund can grow into a permanent, independently governed national asset base.

The long-term objective is straightforward: **build assets once, retain them for the country, and use their returns to build more.**

## Why the NRF?

Britain repeatedly spends enormous sums responding to short-term economic and energy pressures. The NRF proposes a different approach: use a defined capitalisation period to acquire productive assets rather than allowing the proceeds to disappear into general expenditure.

The proposal is built around several principles:

- **Productive investment, not routine spending.** NRF capital is invested into assets intended to generate long-term economic and public value.
- **Energy first.** Generation, storage and grid-supporting infrastructure form the initial investment programme.
- **Technology-neutral procurement.** Storage and generation technologies compete according to cost, performance, longevity, safety, domestic economic value and system benefit.
- **Independent governance.** The statutory structure should create exceptional statutory and institutional barriers to political interference.
- **Finite extraordinary contributions.** The launch capitalisation mechanism is subject to a statutory sunset framework.
- **Permanent national assets.** Ending the launch contributions does not end the Fund: the assets and investment returns remain.
- **UK-wide participation.** Devolved nations and English regions should have meaningful involvement in identifying strategic investment priorities.

## How would it be capitalised?

The proposal has two principal launch streams:

1. A contribution from households with extreme levels of wealth.
2. A contribution from the largest corporate groups based on their **UK Economic Footprint**.

The corporate mechanism is intended to reflect genuine UK economic activity, including that of multinational groups whose accounting profits may not fully reflect their UK footprint. The final legislation would require group-level allocation rules, anti-avoidance provisions and an affordability ceiling based on genuine economic capacity.

Final rates and collectible revenue require HMRC/OBR-quality modelling. Scale benchmarks should not be presented as scored forecasts before that work is complete.

## The 3.5% campaign

The 3.5% figure is an **organising inspiration, not a prediction or guarantee**.

Research into non-violent civil-resistance movements has shown the influence that relatively small but highly engaged sections of a population can sometimes achieve. The campaign uses 3.5% as an ambitious mobilisation target: can millions of people demonstrate enough active support for a serious national investment proposal that political parties have to engage with it?

The campaign is non-party political. Major parties should be invited to provide formal positions on the NRF, and those responses should be published transparently.

## Give a Quid

**If you believe the National Renewal Fund deserves a chance, Give a Quid.**

Give a Quid is intended as a simple grassroots expression of commitment. A £1 contribution is deliberately small and accessible. Its significance comes from the breadth of participation rather than the amount contributed by any one person.

Registered supporters and Give a Quid contributions should be reported separately and transparently. Neither is presented as a scientific opinion poll or a guarantee of voting behaviour.

## Status

The NRF is a policy proposal and grassroots campaign under development. Figures are illustrative unless explicitly identified as independently scored estimates. The project welcomes scrutiny from economists, tax specialists, energy-market experts, infrastructure investors, constitutional specialists and policymakers.

This repository is intended to provide an open public record of the proposal as it develops.
