# National Renewal Fund — Core Proposition

The National Renewal Fund (NRF) is a proposal for a permanent, independently governed UK investment institution designed to build productive national assets, beginning with energy generation, storage and supporting infrastructure.

## The proposition

The UK would establish the NRF in statute and capitalise it for a defined launch period through targeted contributions from extreme private wealth and the largest corporate economic footprints in the UK.

That capital would not be used as ordinary government spending. It would be invested into productive assets capable of generating long-term income and public value.

Income generated by NRF assets would be retained and reinvested, allowing the Fund to compound its asset base over time. The objective is to create an institution designed for long-term fiscal self-sufficiency, subject to the eventual accounting treatment and final statutory design.

## Initial investment focus

Energy is the proposed first investment programme because energy costs, security and infrastructure affect households, businesses and the wider economy.

The NRF would invest across a portfolio rather than prescribe a single technology. Storage would be central to the strategy alongside generation and grid-supporting infrastructure.

Storage tenders should be technology-neutral and assessed against characteristics including:

- cost per MWh delivered;
- discharge duration;
- lifetime cycles;
- degradation;
- domestic supply-chain content;
- safety;
- round-trip efficiency;
- recyclability; and
- potential grid-service revenue.

Technologies including sodium-ion, lithium-ion, flow batteries, pumped storage and future commercially credible alternatives should compete according to their suitability for each application.

## Capitalisation principles

The corporate contribution would be designed around a company's **UK Economic Footprint**, so multinational groups with substantial UK economic activity cannot avoid the contribution simply because taxable profit is booked elsewhere.

The statutory design would require robust group-level rules, allocation methodology, anti-avoidance provisions and an affordability ceiling linked to genuine economic capacity. Profit and cash-flow measures would be used as safeguards against imposing an unsustainable liability, not as elective routes for avoiding the contribution.

A separate contribution from households with extreme levels of wealth would form the other principal capitalisation stream.

Final rates and collectible revenue must be established through HMRC/OBR-quality modelling using the final statutory design. Public figures used before that work is complete should be presented as scale benchmarks rather than scored forecasts.

## A finite contribution, a permanent asset base

The NRF is not intended to create a permanent new contribution simply because the state can collect it.

Its launch contribution mechanism would contain a statutory sunset framework. Once the Fund has reached the defined conditions for sustainable self-financing, the extraordinary capitalisation mechanism would end in accordance with the legislation.

The productive assets, investment income and institution would remain.

## Independence and governance

The NRF should operate under a statutory mandate with professional investment governance, transparent reporting, independent audit and clearly defined investment objectives.

Its structure should **create exceptional statutory and institutional barriers to political interference** while preserving proper Parliamentary accountability.

Governments should not be able to treat NRF assets as a convenient source of short-term spending or redirect the Fund for routine political priorities.

## Relationship to the National Wealth Fund

The existing National Wealth Fund and the proposed National Renewal Fund are not the same proposition.

The National Wealth Fund is an existing government-owned investment institution intended to mobilise investment in support of UK policy objectives. The NRF is proposed as a new, permanent compounding national asset fund with a distinct capitalisation mechanism, statutory sunset for those launch contributions and stronger institutional separation from day-to-day government spending.

The existence of the National Wealth Fund demonstrates that public investment institutions can operate within the UK system. The NRF proposal asks a different question: whether an independently governed national fund could accumulate productive assets at substantially greater scale and retain the resulting returns for long-term national benefit.

## Devolved nations

The NRF is conceived as a UK-wide institution. Its investment framework should provide meaningful participation for Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland and English regions in identifying strategic investment priorities.

Devolved investment programmes could reflect regional strengths and needs — for example storage, wind, hydro and marine opportunities in Scotland — while remaining within the overall statutory and governance framework of the NRF.

## The public proposition

The NRF is ultimately based on a simple idea:

The UK can ask those with the greatest economic capacity to make a finite contribution towards building assets that strengthen the country for decades to come.

Instead of the proceeds disappearing into general expenditure, they would create tangible infrastructure, future investment income, greater energy security and a permanent national asset base.

The objective is not simply to raise money. It is to convert a period of extraordinary contribution into enduring national wealth.
