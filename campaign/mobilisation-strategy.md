# Mobilisation Strategy

The 3.5% campaign exists to build visible, broad-based public support for serious consideration of the National Renewal Fund.

## Organising target

The 3.5% figure is an inspiration and mobilisation target, not a prediction of political outcomes. The campaign should measure its progress through transparent indicators such as registered supporters, Give a Quid contributions, public endorsements and formal engagement from institutions and political parties.

## Campaign stages

### 1. Establish credibility

Publish the core proposition, fiscal framework, governance design, evidence base and major objections openly. Invite expert criticism and amend the proposal where scrutiny identifies genuine weaknesses.

### 2. Build public understanding

Explain individual NRF concepts in accessible language through the website, social media, short videos, graphics, interviews and public discussion. Communications should distinguish evidence, illustrative modelling and policy ambition.

### 3. Convert interest into participation

Invite people to register support and, where they wish, Give a Quid. Publish supporter and contribution figures separately.

### 4. Engage civil society and industry

Seek dialogue with economists, energy specialists, businesses, trade and professional bodies, charities, universities, think tanks and other organisations with relevant expertise.

### 5. Seek formal political positions

Invite major political parties and relevant elected representatives to provide formal positions on the NRF. Publish responses fairly and transparently.

## Non-party political approach

The campaign should not become an adjunct of any single political party. The NRF contains ideas capable of being considered from different political perspectives: public asset ownership, energy security, productive investment, fiscal discipline, regional development and a finite contribution from those with the greatest economic capacity.

The objective is to make the proposal available for adoption, scrutiny or improvement across the political spectrum.

## Geographic strategy

Engagement can begin in devolved nations and regions where particular elements of the investment model have strong relevance. Scotland, for example, has substantial renewable generation, storage and grid opportunities and an existing public investment institution in the Scottish National Investment Bank.

A devolved administration may be able to explore or champion aspects of the NRF investment model even where the full UK-wide capitalisation mechanism would require Westminster legislation.

## Campaign standard

The campaign should avoid threats, artificial deadlines or claims that political change is inevitable at a particular supporter threshold. Its leverage should come from the quality of the proposal, the breadth of genuine public support and the willingness of policymakers to engage with a transparent public case.
