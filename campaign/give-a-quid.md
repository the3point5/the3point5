# Give a Quid

## Purpose

Give a Quid is a grassroots mechanism allowing anyone who supports the National Renewal Fund to contribute £1 to the campaign.

The contribution provides a simple and tangible way for people to demonstrate active support for the proposal while collectively helping to fund the campaign required to promote it.

**If you support the idea, Give a Quid.**

The strength of the model lies in participation rather than the amount contributed by any individual. Giving £1 represents a small but deliberate commitment while keeping participation accessible regardless of personal income or wealth.

At scale, individual contributions can also provide meaningful grassroots campaign funding:

- 100,000 contributions = £100,000
- 1 million contributions = £1 million
- 2.35 million contributions = £2.35 million

## Support and contributions

The campaign should distinguish clearly between:

- **Registered supporters** — people who have actively registered their support for the NRF.
- **Give a Quid contributions** — £1 contributions received in support of the campaign.

Neither measure is a scientific opinion poll, verified electoral register or guarantee of future voting behaviour. They are direct expressions of support for the proposal.

## Proportionate safeguards

The campaign platform should use proportionate measures to protect supporter statistics and payment systems, including email confirmation, bot protection, rate limiting, basic duplicate detection, payment-provider fraud controls and monitoring for abnormal activity.

Payment totals should be reconciled against payment-provider and banking records so published financial figures can be substantiated.

## Privacy

The campaign should follow data-minimisation principles and collect only information reasonably required to operate the campaign, communicate with supporters, process contributions and meet legal obligations.

Supporter information should not be sold or commercially exploited. Public reporting should use aggregated information rather than identifying individual supporters.

## Campaign funds

Give a Quid contributions should be processed through established payment providers and held in appropriate campaign accounts.

Periodic reporting should cover gross contributions, processing and banking costs, campaign expenditure and remaining funds. Larger donations received outside Give a Quid should be separately identifiable and handled in accordance with applicable political-finance requirements.

Larger financial contributions should not provide additional influence over NRF policy or campaign positions.

## The 3.5% target

The 3.5% figure provides an ambitious organising target and identity for the movement. It is inspired by research into non-violent civil-resistance movements and is not a prediction or guarantee that reaching a particular threshold will result in adoption of the NRF.

Progress should report registered supporters and Give a Quid contributions separately.

## Public dashboard

A public dashboard should provide a transparent picture of campaign growth, potentially including:

- registered supporters;
- Give a Quid contributions;
- gross grassroots funding raised;
- campaign expenditure and costs; and
- aggregate geographic support where sufficient data exists.

The methodology behind each published measure should be clearly explained.

## Guiding principle

Give a Quid turns passive agreement into a small act of participation. Its strength comes from the breadth of participation rather than the wealth of individual supporters.

**If you believe the National Renewal Fund deserves a chance, Give a Quid.**
