# Political Party Engagement

The National Renewal Fund campaign should invite major UK political parties to provide a formal position on the proposal.

## Purpose

The objective is not to force an artificial deadline or to align the campaign with one party. It is to create a transparent public record of whether parties are prepared to engage with the NRF and, where they disagree, why.

## Common invitation

Each party should receive the same core policy material and be invited to state whether it:

- supports the NRF in principle;
- would consider adopting it as manifesto policy;
- supports the principle subject to specified amendments; or
- does not support it, together with its reasons.

Responses should be published accurately and without selective editing.

Where no formal response is received by a stated consultation date, the public record should say simply: **No formal response received.**

## Parliamentary engagement

Engagement should be targeted initially towards representatives with relevant portfolios or interests, including energy, Treasury and economic policy, infrastructure, business, poverty, constitutional affairs and devolved government.

The campaign should also seek scrutiny from Parliamentary committees and cross-party groups where appropriate.

## Devolved nations

The campaign should engage MSPs, Members of the Senedd and MLAs on the investment and regional-participation aspects of the NRF.

The full proposed corporate and extreme-wealth capitalisation mechanisms would require the necessary legislative competence. Devolved engagement should therefore distinguish between elements that can be explored under existing devolved powers and elements requiring UK legislation.

A devolved nation could nevertheless become an early advocate or test bed for aspects of the public-investment model, particularly where existing institutions and regional energy opportunities provide a strong foundation.
