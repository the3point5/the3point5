# Revenue Evidence and Capitalisation Potential

## Purpose

The NRF's revenue case distinguishes between **evidence of scale**, **illustrative calculations** and a **formal receipts forecast**.

A formal forecast requires the final statutory design to be modelled against company- and household-level data. Until that work is complete, the figures below demonstrate potential scale rather than claiming a scored launch yield.

## Corporate scale

Department for Business and Trade Business Population Estimates 2025 report approximately **£2.7 trillion** of annual turnover for UK private-sector businesses employing 250 or more people, excluding financial and insurance activities.

A simple 1% calculation on that turnover is approximately **£27 billion**.

This is not a forecast of NRF receipts. The eventual result would be affected by the £25 million group allowance, UK Economic Footprint Revenue allocation, sector coverage, the Adjusted UK Economic Profit affordability ceiling and behavioural effects.

## Extreme-wealth scale

Research by Ben Tippet and Gabriel Zucman has estimated that a 2% annual minimum tax on UK households with wealth above £100 million could raise approximately **£10 billion per year**.

The NRF is structured differently as a limited decadal capital contribution. The research therefore provides evidence of the potential scale of the wealth base rather than a direct NRF revenue estimate.

## Corporate affordability

The corporate contribution is subject to an affordability ceiling of 20% of Adjusted UK Economic Profit.

The ceiling is designed to protect genuinely low-margin activity while preventing ordinary profit-shifting mechanisms from artificially reducing liability. AUEP uses the higher of a UK tax-adjusted operating-profit measure and a statutory share of consolidated group pre-tax profit apportioned according to UK economic activity.

Company-level modelling is required to determine how frequently the ceiling would bind and its effect on receipts.

## Required formal modelling

Before implementation, the final policy should be independently modelled to produce **low, central and high** revenue scenarios covering:

- eligible corporate groups;
- UK-EFR by group;
- the £25 million allowance;
- AUEP and the affordability ceiling;
- sector coverage;
- household wealth valuation and residence rules;
- liquidity provisions;
- behavioural responses;
- administration and compliance costs; and
- interaction with existing UK and international tax rules.

Initial NRF investment envelopes should be tied to verified receipts rather than an assumed headline launch figure.
