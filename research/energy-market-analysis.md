# Energy Market and Storage Strategy

## Storage first

The NRF's first infrastructure priority is **grid-scale energy storage**.

The policy does not prescribe a single winning technology. The objective is to procure the combination of technologies that delivers the best system value, resilience and long-term commercial performance.

Candidate technologies may include sodium-ion batteries, LFP and other battery chemistries, pumped hydro, flow batteries, compressed-air storage, thermal storage and future commercially proven technologies.

## Tender characteristics

NRF storage procurement should be competitive and technology-neutral. Assessment criteria should include:

- whole-life cost per MWh delivered;
- required discharge duration;
- lifetime cycles;
- degradation profile;
- round-trip efficiency;
- safety and insurability;
- recyclability and end-of-life cost;
- UK supply-chain content and resilience;
- construction and commissioning time;
- land, planning and grid-connection requirements;
- operational availability;
- expected grid-service revenues;
- arbitrage potential; and
- compatibility with the wider NRF generation portfolio.

Tender weightings should be published and may vary by the system service being procured.

## Why storage precedes generation

The NRF is intended to add productive infrastructure to the electricity system rather than simply maximise installed generation capacity.

Storage can provide flexibility, absorb surplus generation, reduce curtailment, support frequency and balancing services and improve the utilisation of existing and future renewable generation.

The detailed investment case must quantify these benefits against alternative network and flexibility investments before capital is committed.

## Subsequent phases

Once the initial storage programme is established, the NRF can expand into commercially appropriate renewable generation, electrification and efficiency, followed by interconnection and export opportunities where these strengthen domestic resilience and provide attractive risk-adjusted returns.

## Evidence programme

The technical White Paper should maintain an independently reviewable evidence base covering current and projected UK storage requirements, network constraints and curtailment, storage capex and operating costs, expected revenue stacks, degradation and replacement assumptions, grid-connection availability, planning constraints, domestic supply-chain opportunities and sensitivity to electricity-market reform.

NRF investment decisions should be driven by this evidence rather than technology advocacy.
