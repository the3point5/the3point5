# National Wealth Fund vs National Renewal Fund

The proposed National Renewal Fund is intended to complement rather than duplicate the UK's existing National Wealth Fund (NWF).

| | National Wealth Fund | National Renewal Fund |
|---|---|---|
| Core role | Government-owned policy bank and investor | Dedicated national energy endowment |
| Scope | Multiple strategic sectors | Energy infrastructure |
| Initial capital | Public financial capacity | Finite contributions from extreme wealth and major UK economic footprints |
| Investment model | Financial investment, including loans, equity and guarantees, with a strong role in mobilising private capital | Build, acquire and retain productive energy assets in public ownership |
| Long-term objective | Support growth, investment and government strategic priorities while generating returns | Compound a permanent public energy asset base until it finances its own approved expansion programme |
| External capitalisation | Determined through government funding arrangements | Ends under a defined operational-self-sufficiency Sunset Clause, with an absolute four-cycle backstop |

## Why create a separate NRF?

The NRF has a deliberately narrow mandate. Its purpose is not to become another general development bank, but to create a permanent portfolio of productive energy assets whose operating revenues are reinvested for future infrastructure.

The distinctive proposition is the combination of:

- a dedicated energy-only mandate;
- capitalisation from extreme private wealth and the largest UK economic footprints;
- permanent public ownership of the resulting asset base;
- reinvestment of operating income; and
- automatic termination of the contribution mechanism once independently demonstrated operational self-sufficiency is reached.

## Cooperation rather than duplication

A detailed implementation study should examine where the NRF and NWF could cooperate, co-invest or share expertise while preventing unnecessary overlap.

The existence of the NWF also provides a useful institutional precedent for a publicly owned, professionally managed investment body operating at arm's length from day-to-day government. The NRF proposal applies a different capitalisation mechanism and a much narrower asset mandate.
